## 2. The 10-12 lost ETF scan → #69

**Symptom:** 10-12's stock scan ran 21:35 → 22:08 Taipei (usually ~5–15 min). At 22:00 the log says
`ETF scan skipped: another scan is already running` — the lock doing its job, a whole session's ETF
cohort gone.

**Cause, measured from the log:** Gemini returned `200 OK` but each call took **60–124 s** (median gap
between completions 39 s that day vs 6–14 s on every other session). The universe build was normal
(~3.5 min both Mondays). On 10-14 one call took **~375 s**; that stock scan finished 4 min before
the ETF slot. A slow *success* is invisible to tenacity and to the model breaker — both act on
failures. No client passed `timeout=`, so requests inherited the SDK default: verified
`Timeout(connect=5.0, read=600, ...)`. #65's `max_retries=0` bounds request *count*, not duration.

**Fix (#69):** timeout passed to all six constructors in `analyst/claude_analyst.py`;
`_should_retry` refuses `openai.APITimeoutError` / `anthropic.APITimeoutError` (503s still retried);
`<= 0` fails startup (SDKs read 0 as *no* deadline). Rationale is in CLAUDE.md *Key Design
Decisions*, next to the model breaker. **Option chosen by the operator** over a scan-level deadline,
"both", or leaving it.

**Accepted cost:** a slow-but-valid primary answer now becomes a fallback call, possibly to the
**paid** DeepSeek tier. 60 s was chosen because normal calls finish in a few seconds; it is a
judgement, not a measurement — the log records only request *completion*, not start, so true
per-request latency was not measurable from it.

**Not changed:** `scripts/probe_analyst_models.py` builds its own client with no timeout (manual tool).
