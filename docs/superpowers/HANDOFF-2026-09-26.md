### 1b. The 503s are Google's capacity, not our rate — §4.5 of 09-24 is closed

**The first Gemini request of every session was a 503** (09-23, 09-24 and 09-25). On 09-24,
`gemini-3.7-flash`'s first request of the day was a 503 too, and nothing else calls that model. A
refusal that arrives before we have sent any traffic cannot be caused by our traffic. **When two
mechanisms could produce the same totals, look for the one event only one of them can produce.**

### 1c. The open decision: a per-scan, per-model breaker

| session | what happened |
|---|---|
| 09-24 | **Total outage: 171 of 172 Gemini requests failed.** All 38 analysed tickers went primary ×3 → fallback ×3 → DeepSeek, so every one of the 38 answers came from the **paid** tier, after roughly 10 minutes of back-off. `gemini-3.7-flash` spent its 20 RPD on 503s and answered **429** after that: 27 of the 32 429s follow a `Primary analyst failed` line. |
| 09-25 | **Partial outage: 37 of 72 requests succeeded**, and only 4 tickers fell back. Retrying *helped* here. |

So "never retry a 503" is wrong. Tenacity asks whether *this request* is worth trying again; on 09-24
the right question was whether *this model* had been failing all scan.

**Proposal:** after a model has exhausted its retries on K consecutive tickers (K = 2 or 3), skip it
for the rest of the scan. This is the news chain's day breaker, scoped to one scan.
- On 09-24 it would have saved about 150 wasted requests and most of the 10 minutes, and it would
  have stopped the fallback's budget burning before any ticker could use it.
- On 09-25 it would never have tripped.
- **It does NOT reduce DeepSeek spend on an outage day.** If every Google model is down, the paid
  tier gets those calls either way.

Build it TDD on a branch if the owner says yes.
