## 1. Cleanups that were not cleanups

### `SCAN_TIMEZONE` — the ETF scan never ran during market hours

`ETF_SCAN_HOUR=9 / ETF_SCAN_MINUTE=30` was written to mean the opening bell. With
`SCAN_TIMEZONE` unset it meant **09:30 Asia/Taipei = 21:30 ET**, 5.5 hours after the close, for
the entire life of the ETF scan. The stock scan's times had been hand-converted to Taipei
(`21:45,3:30` = 09:45/15:30 ET) and the ETF scan's never were, so the two ran on two different
clocks while looking like they shared one.

A second, dated bug rode along: Taipei does not observe DST and the US does, so on
**2026-11-01** the hand-converted stock times would have slid to **08:45 EST — 45 minutes before
the open** — silently, for the whole winter.

`.env` is migrated to `SCAN_TIMES=09:45,15:30` + `SCAN_TIMEZONE=America/New_York`. Verified
against the real `CronTrigger` across the DST boundary, not by hand arithmetic. A backup of the
old file is at `.env.bak-pre-timezone`.

**Both halves must move together.** Setting the timezone alone reinterprets the hand-converted
local times as market times and fires the scans at 03:30 and 21:45 ET, both with the market
shut — worse than leaving it unset. `.env.example` now says so.

### The ETF scan never swept terminal orders

`_run_scan_locked` called `sweep_terminal_recommendations` before building its universe;
`_run_scan_etf_locked` never swept at all. Both paths post recommendations and share one lock,
so either can be the scan that discovers an order went terminal. Fixed, with the test
instrumenting `partition_watchlist` — **not** `get_universe`, which the ETF path never calls and
which is how a sibling test passed vacuously last cycle.

### The probe tool reproduced both traps it existed to prevent

`scripts/probe_analyst_models.py` hardcoded `time.sleep(1.5)` — **~40 RPM** against a chain
capped at 5–15 RPM — and hardcoded the Gemini base URL, so probing the deepseek tier posted a
deepseek model id to Google and reported the resulting error as a failure *of the model*.

Rewritten: delay defaults to `ANALYST_CALL_DELAY_S` and a smaller `--delay` is **refused**;
client construction goes through the app's own `_OPENAI_BASE_URLS`; with no arguments it probes
all **three** tiers. 8 new tests.

**Known limitation, bit me the same day:** the delay floor is per-**config**, not per-**model**.
`ANALYST_CALL_DELAY_S=4.0` is calibrated for the 15 RPM primary, so probing a 5 RPM model at the
floor still reproduces trap 2. **Pass `--delay 12` explicitly for any `*-flash` model.**

## 4. ⚠️ `gemini-3.7-flash` may not belong in the chain

Probed after its quota reset, at **12s pacing (5 RPM, its documented cap — so trap 2 is ruled
out this time)**:

```
gemini-3.7-flash    3/6 parsed  (50%)
  1x HTTP 503                      <- ambiguous as always
  2x PARSE FAILURE                 <- NOT ambiguous
     "Response missing SIGNAL: line"
     "Response missing REASONING: line"
```

**The parse failures are clean HTTP 200s carrying unreadable text.** That is exactly the failure
mode `gemma-4-31b-it` was banned for, and exactly what PR #32 installed 3.7-flash to fix.

For comparison, measured through the same tool and parser:

| model | result |
|---|---|
| `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` (primary) | 9/9 + 6/6 |
| `deepseek-v4-flash` (fallback2) | **6/6 — first measurement ever**; the old tool could not reach it |
| `gemini-3.7-flash` (fallback) | **3/6** |

A second n=9 probe DID complete. **Its availability numbers are worthless and its parse
numbers are not** — read the distinction carefully:

```
probe 2 (n=9):  2x PARSE FAILURE, 2x 503, 2x 429 ("You exceeded your...")
```

**The 429s mean I exhausted the daily budget while probing.** 6 calls in probe 1 + 9 in probe 2
= 15 against a 20 RPD cap, and every failed call burns `@_retry` attempts on top. So probe 2's
503/429s are a spent budget, NOT model instability — the same trap this path has now hit three
times, and this time I walked into it myself.

**What survives:** parse failures are not a quota artifact. A clean HTTP 200 carrying unreadable
text is a quality signal regardless of budget. Across both probes, `gemini-3.7-flash` produced
**4 parse failures** ("Response missing SIGNAL: line", "Response missing REASONING: line").

**Do NOT re-probe 3.7-flash today** — the budget is gone and you will only manufacture more
429s. After the next **00:00 PT** reset:

```bash
.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/probe_analyst_models.py gemini:gemini-3.7-flash --repeat 2 --delay 12
```

Keep it to `--repeat 2` (6 calls). At 20 RPD there is no room for a large sample, which is itself
part of the argument against this model occupying the fallback slot.

**Decision needed, not yet made.** The evidence now: 4 parse failures across ~15 attempts on
`gemini-3.7-flash`, versus 15/15 for the primary and 6/6 for `deepseek-v4-flash`. The fallback
tier looks like mostly dead weight, and deepseek is the better second rung — different provider,
separate quota pool, and no 20 RPD ceiling forcing every measurement to be underpowered.

Counter-argument worth stating: n is small, and a fallback only fires when the primary fails, so
a mediocre fallback still beats no fallback. The change is cheap either way (two `.env` lines),
and `degenerate_fallback_reason()` already guards against the fallback silently collapsing onto
the primary. **This is the user's call, not something to change unprompted.**
