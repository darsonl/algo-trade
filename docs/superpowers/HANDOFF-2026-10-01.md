## 1. The breaker (#66)

A model that fails **3 consecutive tickers** within one scan is skipped for the rest of that scan
(`ANALYST_BREAKER_THRESHOLD`, 0 disables). The rationale is in CLAUDE.md *Key Design Decisions* and
`analyst/model_breaker.py`, and CLAUDE.md wins if the two ever disagree.

**K was chosen by replay.** For every scan 09-24..09-30, the answering model per ticker in scan order
comes from `shadow_observations.analyst_model` (`P` primary, `F` 3.7-flash, `D` DeepSeek):

| session | kind | sequence |
|---|---|---|
| 09-24 | stock | `DDPDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDD` |
| 09-24 | etf | `DDDDDDD` |
| 09-25 | stock | `FPPPPPDPPPPPPPPPPPPDPPDPPPPPPPPPPPPP` |
| 09-28 | stock | `PPPPPPPPDPPPPPDPPPPPPPPPPPPPPPPFDPPFPP` |
| 09-28 | etf | `PPPPPDPDDP` |
| 09-29, 09-30 | both | all `P` |

With K=3 there are **0 answerable tickers skipped**. With K=2 there are 7, 6 of them on 09-28 stock,
where the primary failed twice and recovered. One caveat: `D` does not distinguish a fallback
*request* failure from a fallback *parse* error, and the breaker counts only the former. The replay
therefore over-counts trips, so "0 lost" is conservative.

**What to watch:** the first outage session. Look for the log line
`failed 3 tickers in a row -- skipping it for the rest of this scan`, then compare the Gemini request
count against the AI Studio dashboard. That comparison is the check that proves the saving, because
the unit tests only prove the mechanism.
