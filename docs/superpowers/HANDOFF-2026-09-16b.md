## 2. What happened, and the reasoning not in the code

### 2a. Why the 09-16 news result is proof, not merely a good sign

The decisive query returning 0 rows is weak evidence on its own — a `LIKE` pattern that never
matches anything also returns 0. Two things make it conclusive.

**The checker was validated against a session known to carry the confound.** Run against
2026-09-15 it fails with **exactly 25 rows** — Alpha Vantage's free-tier budget, spent in scan
order, which is the precise step function the diagnosis predicted. It failed with the predicted
*signature*, not merely failed.

**The chain's failure modes leave a trace either way.** If Finnhub had failed for any ticker, the
chain falls through to Alpha Vantage, which either succeeds (stamping a `[Label, score]` suffix
into `headlines_json`) or fails (writing a refusal line to the log). The 09-16 session has
**neither** — 0 suffixes across 50 rows, 0 AV log lines, 0 Finnhub failures. Therefore Finnhub
served all 35 tickers that reached the analyst. That is uniformity established by elimination
rather than by absence.

### 2b. #61 — the ETF path stored the one price source the rule forbids

`CLAUDE.md` has said since the screen-price work that `reference_price` comes from
`screen_price(info)` at every post-`.info` exit and **never** from the technical stage. The ETF
loop stored `tech_data["price"]` — `closes.iloc[-1]`, a different endpoint fetched minutes later,
auto-adjusted where `.info` is raw — and recorded **no `reference_price_source` at all**. `.info`
was already in hand in that loop, fetched for the expense ratio.

**The quota exit was the serious half, and the least visible.** `skipped_quota_exhausted` carried
no price at all. `pending_shadow_marks` requires `reference_price > 0`, so an unpriced row is
silently excluded from the marking queue and appears in no outcome analysis. Whether an ETF was
*measurable at all* therefore depended on whether the analyst budget ran out before reaching it —
and exhaustion follows scan order, which is not random. **A wrong price is visibly wrong; a
missing one removes the row from the denominator without leaving a trace.**

**Provenance was legible in the stored digits.** pandas holds OHLC history as float32, so the
history close widens into a float64 that cannot represent the value cleanly — ETF rows carried
`760.1900024414062` and `71.84500122070312` where stock rows from `.info` carried `422.49` and
`1147.989`. Worth carrying elsewhere: **when auditing which of two sources produced a column, look
at the representation, not only at a provenance field** — it survives even when that field is NULL.

**Deliberately unchanged, each verified against the stock path rather than assumed:**

- The recommendation row and embed keep `tech_data["price"]`. The stock path does the same
  (`main.py:973`, `:987`). That is the decision/display price — what a human would have transacted
  at — which is a different question from the research column. Changing it would have been scope
  creep dressed as consistency.
- The pre-`.info` skips and the `error` exit stay unpriced: `.info` never existed for them.
- **No backfill.** ETF observations store no `fundamentals_json` (verified 30/30 NULL), so
  `backfill_screen_price.py` has nothing to recover from. The fix is necessarily forward-only.

## 3. Found and deliberately LEFT — the owner's call was "just fix item 1"

Both change what gets posted, so both are rule boundaries needing a measured case first, on the
dividend-floor precedent. Recorded so they are not re-discovered from scratch.

1. **`ETF_MAX_EXPENSE_RATIO` gates nothing.** It appears in exactly three places: the config
   definition, the embed call, and `discord_bot/embeds.py:96`, where it picks a *warning colour*.
   A 0.95% ETF is recommended with a badge, never filtered. This is the `paper_trading` shape
   `CLAUDE.md` already names — read in one place, reads like a safety layer, decides nothing.
2. **ETFs have no gate at all.** `gate_config_json` and `technical_verdict` are NULL on every ETF
   row, so the analyst is their only filter. The preregistration's question — "what would a
   pipeline without the analyst have posted?" — has no answer for ETFs *by construction*, because
   there is nothing to compare against.
